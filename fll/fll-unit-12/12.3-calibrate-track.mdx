# Lesson F12.3: Calibrate Axle Track

Once straight travel is calibrated, a full turn can help estimate axle track. Pybricks recommends a 360 degree turn on a clear surface. If the robot repeatedly turns too far, a smaller configured track may help; a short turn may call for a larger one.

## Worked example

The example uses a 375 degree actual turn for a 360 degree request to calculate a smaller starting track. A single turn is an estimate, so the result still needs repeated checks.

~~~python
old_track_mm = 112
requested_deg = 360
actual_deg = 375
new_track_mm = old_track_mm * requested_deg / actual_deg
print("Try axle track mm:", new_track_mm)
~~~

## Simulator practice

Replace the starter's straight drive with a 360 degree turn and compare headings in the automatic profile table. After changing axle track in code, the same route can show whether the difference shrinks.

## On the robot

Three full turns from the same floor mark may show whether the error repeats. Small track adjustments can then be checked against both turns and straight distance.

## Check and adjust

The selected track should improve the turns without undoing the straight calibration. If results vary between trials, grip and chassis structure may need attention before another numerical change.
