# Lesson F0.M2: What Simple Machines Do

A **simple machine** changes the direction, size, or location of an applied force. It can make a motion easier to control, but it does not create energy. A longer movement may trade for a stronger push, or a stronger push may trade for a shorter movement.

| Simple machine | What it does | LEGO robot example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lever** | Rotates around a pivot to move a load | A short arm lifts a handle. |
| **Wheel and axle** | Turns an axle and wheel together | Drive wheels move the robot; a gear fixed to an attachment axle passes rotation along. |
| **Pulley** | Guides a string or changes its pulling direction | A string routed over a pulley raises a light object. |
| **Inclined plane** | Spreads a lift over a longer path | A ramp guides an object upward. |
| **Wedge** | Pushes things apart or guides them sideways | A tapered guide centers an object as the robot approaches. |
| **Screw** | Turns rotation into travel along a thread | A threaded LEGO part can move a small piece gradually. |

Gears are often used with wheels and axles to change speed, turning direction, or torque. A mechanism can combine several simple machines, such as a motor, gears, axle, and lever arm. For FLL attachments, levers, wheels and axles, ramps, and wedges are often the easiest places to start.

## Hands-on practice

Build a lever from a beam and a pivot. Move the pivot closer to one end and compare how far each end travels. Then turn a wheel fixed to an axle and watch how both rotate together. Draw arrows showing the input and output motion of each build. Without a LEGO set, a ruler on a pencil can demonstrate the lever, and a cardboard circle on a pencil can demonstrate the wheel and axle.

## Check and adjust

Name the simple machine in each build and explain what changed: direction, distance, force, or control. If a mechanism slips or binds, inspect its pivot and axle supports before adding a larger motor.
