# Lesson F0.4: Turn and Steer

`DriveBase.turn()` rotates the robot in place: positive angles turn right and negative angles turn left. A turn depends on axle track and wheel grip, while a curve adds forward movement, so confirm a 90 degree right turn before testing a curve or changing speed.

## Worked example

~~~python
robot.straight(200)
robot.turn(90)
robot.straight(50)
robot.turn(-90)
~~~

## Simulator practice

Run the route. Change the second turn to 90 degrees, then predict the final heading before running again.

## On the robot

Use tape to mark the robot's starting heading. Test a 90 degree turn three times and record the actual angle.

## Check and adjust

You can predict the heading after each command and explain why an encoder turn may differ on the mat. If a turn misses its mark, measure axle track and wheel contact points.
