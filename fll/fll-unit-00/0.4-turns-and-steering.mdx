# Lesson F0.4: Turn and Steer

After driving a measured distance, we can change the robot's heading with `robot.turn()`. A positive angle requests a right turn and a negative angle requests a left turn. This lesson uses two turns to check where the robot faces after a short route. On a physical robot, axle track and wheel grip may affect the angle reached.

## Worked example

The first turn points the robot to its right for a 50 mm drive. The second turn points it back toward its original heading. If both turns are accurate, the route should end facing the same direction it started.

~~~python
robot.straight(200)
robot.turn(90)
robot.straight(50)
robot.turn(-90)
~~~

## Simulator practice

The simulator shows the heading after each segment. With `robot.turn(-90)` at the end, the two turns cancel. Replacing that call with `robot.turn(90)` should leave the robot facing a different direction. A prediction before the second run can be checked against the field view.

## On the robot

A tape mark can give the robot the same starting heading for three 90 degree right turns. Comparing the final headings may show a repeatable offset or variation caused by grip and alignment.

## Check and adjust

The final heading should follow from the two commanded turns. If the physical turn misses the mark in the same direction each time, the axle track value and wheel contact points are worth checking before changing the route.
