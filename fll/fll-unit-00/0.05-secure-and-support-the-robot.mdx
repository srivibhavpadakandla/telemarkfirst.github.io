# Lesson F0.M5: Secure and Support the Robot

A mechanism works only when its shape stays the same from one run to the next. Before programming, secure the base robot and every attachment so a bump or turn cannot shift the hub, loosen a wheel, or change the tool's contact point.

## Build checks

- **Make the robot frame rigid.** Join the left and right sides with cross beams. Attach the hub and motors firmly to the frame, and make sure the battery and cables cannot snag a model or wheel.
- **Secure the attachment.** Connect it at two spaced points where possible. Check that pins are fully seated and that the tool cannot twist loose when pushed sideways. Make swaps easy to repeat at the same position.
- **Avoid cantilevers.** A **cantilever** sticks out from one support with a load at its free end. It bends and twists more easily. Do not leave a wheel, axle, motor, or mission tool hanging from one unsupported side. Place a second frame support on the far side of an axle or arm, shorten the overhang, or add a brace when the design needs reach.
- **Keep motion clear.** Turn every wheel and moving attachment by hand. Nothing should rub, bind, fall off, or hit the hub through its full travel.

## Quick reliability test

Lift the robot by the frame, set it down, and gently push the attachment in the direction it will meet the model. Its height and angle should stay the same. Run the mechanism three times from the same start mark. If it works once and fails twice, inspect loose connections and unsupported overhangs before changing the program.

## Check and adjust

Point to the path that carries force from the contact point back into the robot frame. If that path ends at a single pin or a long unsupported beam, add support and test again. You are ready for the programming lessons when the mechanism stays aligned through repeated runs.
