# Lesson F0.M5: Secure and Support the Robot

A tool may reach the model once and miss it on the next run if its support moves. Before programming, we can check whether the robot frame and attachment keep the same shape under a gentle push. The aim is a contact point that stays in place across repeated starts.

## Build checks

Cross beams can help the left and right sides of the frame move together. The hub, motors, battery, and cables should stay clear of wheels and field models. Two spaced attachment points may keep a tool from twisting when it is pushed sideways, and fully seated pins make a swap easier to repeat.

A **cantilever** extends from one support with a load at its free end. A long unsupported wheel axle or tool may bend or twist. A second frame support, a shorter overhang, or a brace can help when the design needs reach. Turning each wheel and moving part by hand may reveal rubbing or binding before a powered test.

## Quick reliability test

After lifting and setting down the robot by its frame, the tool should return to the same height and angle. A gentle push in the model's contact direction can show whether it twists. Three trials from one start mark may reveal movement that a single successful run hides. If the results vary, inspect loose connections and unsupported overhangs before changing the program.

## Check and adjust

The load from the contact point needs a path back into the frame. If that path depends on one pin or a long unsupported beam, another support may improve repeatability. The mechanism is ready for the programming lessons when repeated runs keep it aligned.
