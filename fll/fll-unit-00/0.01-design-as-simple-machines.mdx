# Lesson F0.M1: Design with Simple Machines

Before choosing LEGO pieces or writing code, we can describe what the mission model needs to do. The aim here is to identify the input from the robot, the motion that input causes, and the output that earns the mission result. A motor's rotation or the robot's push may pass through several simple machines before the model moves.

For example, a motor may turn an axle that moves a short arm against a handle. Sketching that chain can show where the robot should touch the model and which part needs to move.

## Try the design lens

Choose a practice model or make a small LEGO target. A drawing of its start and goal positions can help you decide whether it needs a push, pull, lift, rotation, or release. Mark a possible contact point and show the direction of the robot's force with an arrow. Then sketch a tool that could make that movement.

If the tool needs many moving parts, look again at the model's required motion. A simpler contact surface may be enough for the first test.

## Check and adjust

Your sketch should show the input, the motion through the tool, and the model's output. A teammate who has not seen your design may be able to point to the contact surface and predict what will move. If they cannot, the contact point or motion arrow may need another look.
