# Lesson F0.M4: Build a Mission Mechanism

The mission breakdown from F0.M3 gives us a contact point and a required motion. This lesson turns that sketch into one attachment we can test. The drive wheels may provide enough input for a fixed pusher or guide; a separate motor becomes useful when the tool must move without the robot driving.

## Build and test one action

First decide whether the input will be a drive, a turn, or an attachment axle. A short lever could rotate a handle; a broad pusher or tapered guide could move a sliding object. The tool should reach the model before the hub or frame does and should leave room for the model's full motion.

One sturdy version is enough for a first test. Support the tool at more than one point on the frame, then move the robot slowly through the action by hand. Repeating three trials from the same launch position can show whether it contacts, moves, and releases the target in a repeatable way.

For a rotating handle, a short beam across its travel path may be enough. A forward drive would push the beam against the handle. If the beam slides off, changing its height or adding a small guide may help before adding another motor.

If you do not have a robot yet, a paper or cardboard mockup can still test the contact point and travel by hand.

## Check and adjust

The input, simple machine, contact point, and output should be visible in the build or sketch. If a trial fails, note the first missed contact or movement. Changing one dimension at a time can help you tell which adjustment made a difference.
