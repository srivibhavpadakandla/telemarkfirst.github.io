# Lesson F0.M4: Build a Mission Mechanism

Use the mission breakdown from F0.M3 to build the simplest attachment that makes the required movement. A robot's wheels already provide a wheel and axle input. You may only need a fixed pusher, a wedge shaped guide, or a small lever to turn that drive motion into the model's action. Add an attachment motor only when the tool needs to move separately from the robot.

## Build and test one action

1. **Choose the input.** Will the robot drive forward, turn, or rotate an attachment axle?
2. **Choose a simple machine.** For a handle that rotates, try a short lever. For an object that must slide, try a wide pusher or tapered guide.
3. **Place the contact point.** Make the tool reach the model without the hub, wheels, or frame hitting it first. Check the model's full range of motion.
4. **Build one sturdy version.** Attach the tool to the frame at more than one point. Keep its contact surface easy to see during a slow test.
5. **Test by hand, then on the field.** Move the robot through the action slowly. Record whether it contacts the target, moves it far enough, and releases cleanly. Run three trials from the same launch position.

For example, to rotate a model's handle, put a short beam across its travel path. Driving forward makes the beam push the handle. If the beam slides off, adjust its height or add a small guide before adding another motor.

If you do not have a robot yet, make a paper or cardboard mockup of the model and tool. Move the pieces by hand and record the contact point and travel before building with LEGO parts.

## Check and adjust

Show a teammate your input, simple machine, contact point, and output. Note the first step that fails in a trial. Change one dimension at a time so you can tell what improved the result.
