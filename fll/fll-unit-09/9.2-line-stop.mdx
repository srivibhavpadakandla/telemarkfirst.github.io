# Lesson F9.2: Stop at a Dark Line

With a measured reflection threshold, the robot can stop when the sensor reaches a dark line. `DriveBase.drive()` keeps moving while the program checks `ColorSensor.reflection()`, so the loop should also have a timeout. We will compare two speeds to see how far the robot travels after detecting the line.

## Worked example

The loop ends when reflection falls to 35 or below, or when three seconds pass. `robot.stop()` is reached after either outcome; the printed distance alone does not identify which condition ended the loop.

~~~python
watch = StopWatch()
robot.drive(100, 0)
while color_sensor.reflection() > 35 and watch.time() < 3000:
    wait(10)
robot.stop()
print("Stop mm:", robot.distance())
~~~

## Simulator practice

Use the worked example's timed line stop at 100 mm/s, then change the drive speed to 200 mm/s. The final distance and sensor position can show how speed affects the stop.

## On the robot

The gap between sensor and wheel contact point should be measured on the real build. Repeated stops at both speeds can show whether the robot needs a slower approach or an offset.

## Check and adjust

The robot should stop whether it finds the line or reaches the time limit. If it passes the desired mark, reducing speed or allowing for sensor position may improve the result.
