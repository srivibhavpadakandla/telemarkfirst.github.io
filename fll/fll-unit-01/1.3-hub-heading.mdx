# Lesson F1.3: Hub Heading and Reset

After stopping at a line or an obstacle, a heading reading can help us check the robot's direction. The aim in this lesson is to reset heading to zero, command a 90 degree right turn, and compare the reading with that command.

The Prime Hub IMU reports heading in degrees relative to its starting or reset direction. Positive values mean clockwise turns. The hub should stay fixed in a known orientation so the reading describes the robot's turn. Heading should be reset while the robot is stopped. If the DriveBase is using gyro control, use `robot.reset(angle=0)` instead of `hub.imu.reset_heading(0)`.

## Worked example

The starter setup does not enable gyro control, so this example resets the IMU directly. `robot.stop()` runs first, and `hub.imu.reset_heading(0)` makes the current direction zero. After `robot.turn(90)`, the final line prints the IMU's estimate of the turn.

~~~python
robot.stop()
hub.imu.reset_heading(0)
robot.turn(90)
print("Heading:", hub.imu.heading())
~~~

## Simulator practice

The starter runs the right turn and prints its heading. The field view also shows the robot's direction. A second run with `robot.turn(-90)` should give a negative reading after the reset. Comparing both readings with the field view can help you check the sign of each turn.

## On the robot

The hub should remain fixed to the robot during these tests. A mark on the practice mat can give you the same starting direction for three right turns. Comparing each printed heading with 90 degrees may reveal a repeatable error or variation between runs. If the sign is unexpected, check how the hub is mounted.

## Check and adjust

The heading should be positive after a right turn and negative after a left turn. The difference between a reading and the commanded angle gives the error for that run. One close result may not tell you how the next turn will end, so compare all three trials.
