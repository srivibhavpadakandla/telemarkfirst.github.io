# Lesson F1.4: Attachment Motors and Objects

We can now use sensors to stop the robot and check its heading after a turn. The next piece is the attachment that will handle a mission object. The aim in this lesson is to rotate its motor through a known angle and bring it back to its starting position.

`Motor.run_angle(speed, rotation_angle)` takes speed in degrees per second and a relative rotation in motor degrees. A positive angle turns the motor one way; a negative angle reverses it. Which way the tool moves depends on how the motor and gears are mounted.

## Worked example

The first call asks the motor to turn 90 degrees at 200 degrees per second. The second asks it to turn back by the same amount. If the mechanism moves freely, the tool should return close to where it started. A gear train may make the tool's angle different from the motor's angle.

~~~python
attachment = Motor(Port.E)
attachment.run_angle(200, 90)
attachment.run_angle(200, -90)
~~~

## Simulator practice

The starter contains the two motor moves from the example. In the simulator, the attachment should rise and return. Watching its motion can help you check the direction and size of the command before using the tool near the red cargo in the next challenge.

## On the robot

The real tool should be tested with the robot lifted so it can move freely. Short moves of 30, 60, and 90 motor degrees can help you find an angle that reaches the object without pressing against the frame or a hard stop. The angle you use in the mission route should come from those tests.

## Check and adjust

A useful angle should let the tool return without striking the frame or holding against a hard stop. If it binds, reduce the travel and inspect the clearance before trying a faster move. The motor angle alone may not tell you where the tool ends up.

Reference: [Pybricks Motor reference](https://docs.pybricks.com/en/stable/pupdevices/motor.html).
