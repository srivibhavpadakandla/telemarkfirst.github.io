# Lesson F5.3: Wait and Measure Time

We have used `wait(10)` to space sensor readings. `StopWatch.time()` gives us a way to measure how long a drive takes in milliseconds. In this lesson, the distance stays at 300 mm while we compare the time at two speeds. A long blocking wait would delay the next sensor check.

## Worked example

The watch starts before `robot.straight(300)`. The print line reports the elapsed time when that move finishes, and `wait(100)` then pauses for another 100 ms. Because the print comes first, its value does not include that final pause.

~~~python
watch = StopWatch()
robot.straight(300)
elapsed_ms = watch.time()
print("Elapsed ms:", elapsed_ms)
wait(100)
~~~

## Simulator practice

Use the worked example in place of the starter's three short moves. The same 300 mm drive should take different simulated times at different straight speeds; keeping the distance fixed makes the time comparison clearer.

## On the robot

Five physical run times with the battery state recorded beside each one may show a wider range than the simulator. The longest time can be a cautious number for match planning.

## Check and adjust

The printed time should describe the drive before the last 100 ms wait. Dividing it by 1000 gives seconds. If the number seems too large, check where the watch starts and which waits occur before the print.
