# Lesson F3.5: Data Check Challenge

This challenge brings together a named distance, a unit conversion, and a drive. The simulator checks that a Python variable reaches `robot.straight()`, the robot finishes near the 300 mm target, and the route avoids a collision. Printing the planned and estimated distances can help explain a miss.

## Worked example

`target_cm * 10` converts 30 cm to 300 mm. After the drive, `robot.distance()` reports the DriveBase estimate. Subtracting the target gives an encoder-based error, which may differ from the physical distance measured on a mat.

~~~python
target_cm = 30
target_mm = target_cm * 10
robot.reset()
robot.straight(target_mm)
error_mm = robot.distance() - target_mm
print("target mm:", target_mm)
print("encoder error mm:", error_mm)
~~~

## Simulator practice

Changing `target_cm` should change the converted value without editing `robot.straight(target_mm)`. The check for a final position near 300 mm will pass only when the chosen value brings the robot back near that target.

## On the robot

Three physical drives from the same mark can give you both encoder estimates and ruler measurements. Comparing them may reveal wheel slip even when the printed encoder error is small.

## Check and adjust

For a 30 cm plan, the printed target should be 300 mm. The log should keep encoder error separate from the difference measured with a ruler. A small encoder error alongside a physical miss may point to slip or an incorrect wheel diameter.
