# Lesson F2.4: Mission Order, Recovery, and Time

The Robot Game lasts 150 seconds, including launches and attachment changes. We can time a short mission and use that result to decide what may fit after it. The aim here is a mission order with room to return or recover if the first attempt takes longer than expected.

## Worked example

`StopWatch()` begins timing before the drive and attachment move. The final print reports the program's elapsed milliseconds after the return command. This does not include the team's time between launches, so a match plan needs a separate allowance for that work.

~~~python
watch = StopWatch()
robot.straight(300)
attachment.run_angle(200, 90)
robot.straight(-300)
print("Mission ms:", watch.time())
~~~

## Simulator practice

The simulator can time two candidate routes. Adding a five-second setup allowance per launch gives a fuller estimate of each plan. A route with fewer chances to miss may be a better first choice even if its simulated score is lower.

## On the robot

Five physical runs with the same attachment can show how much the route time varies. The median and longest time give different planning numbers; the longest run may be more useful when deciding how much recovery time to reserve.

## Check and adjust

A usable plan should fit within 150 seconds after its setup and recovery allowances. If the estimate exceeds that limit, dropping an objective may leave enough time for a return. The plan should state which objective the team would skip if time runs short.
