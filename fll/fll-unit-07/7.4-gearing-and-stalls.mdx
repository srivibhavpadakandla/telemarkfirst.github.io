# Lesson F7.4: Gearing, Load, and Stalls

Gears can change how far the tool moves for one motor turn and how much torque reaches it. Pybricks can account for a known gear train through the Motor's `gears` setting. This lesson compares commanded angles with visible travel, then introduces a carefully limited way to find a physical home position. The simulator shows a simplified tool and cannot test binding.

## Worked example

The commented lines are for a physical mechanism after its safe travel has been checked. They show a gear list and a low duty limit for `run_until_stalled()` before resetting the angle. The active line is a measured 60 degree move that the simulator can show.

~~~python
# On real hardware, after checking the mechanism:
# attachment = Motor(Port.E, gears=[12, 36])
# attachment.run_until_stalled(-100, duty_limit=30)
# attachment.reset_angle(0)
attachment.run_angle(120, 60)
~~~

## Simulator practice

The virtual attachment can show how a 60 degree command differs from 90 degrees. It should not be used to decide how much force a real attachment can safely take at a stop.

## On the robot

The real gear train and output travel should be inspected with the robot off. If the hardware-only homing lines are used, the first test should be supervised with a low duty limit and a clear way to stop it.

## Check and adjust

A recorded safe angle range should come from physical tests, not the virtual tool. If the attachment binds during an ordinary move, inspect the gears and supports before relying on stall-based homing.
