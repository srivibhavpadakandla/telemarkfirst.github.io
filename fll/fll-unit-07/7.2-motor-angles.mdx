# Lesson F7.2: Relative Tool Angles

`Motor.run_angle(speed, rotation_angle)` moves relative to the current motor position, so two positive 90 degree moves add roughly 180 degrees. Record the angle after each push and return. A blocked tool may stop short of the requested angle and change where the next mission begins.

## Worked example

~~~python
start_deg = attachment.angle()
attachment.run_angle(180, 90)
print("After push:", attachment.angle())
attachment.run_angle(180, -90)
print("Back at:", attachment.angle(), "start:", start_deg)
~~~

## Simulator practice

Compare the final tool angle with start_deg. Change the first move to 60 degrees and predict the result.

## On the robot

Repeat the push and return three times. Inspect clearance and whether the tool drifts away from its marked home angle.

## Check and adjust

The final angle is near the starting angle and the tool clears the frame. If it does not return home, compare angles after both relative moves.
