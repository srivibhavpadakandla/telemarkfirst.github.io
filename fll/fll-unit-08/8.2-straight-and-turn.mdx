# Lesson F8.2: Straight and Turn Segments

A route can be built from short straight and turn segments. Each command finishes before the next starts, so we can compare the robot's position with a planned corner after every segment. A negative distance reverses, and a negative angle turns left. This lesson uses those signs to locate the first part of a route that misses.

## Worked example

The reset gives the route a known distance and angle reading. The printed values describe the movement after the three segments, although they do not by themselves tell us whether the robot reached a field mark.

~~~python
robot.reset()
robot.straight(300)
robot.turn(90)
robot.straight(50)
print("Distance mm:", robot.distance())
print("Angle deg:", robot.angle())
~~~

## Simulator practice

Use the worked route to mark a predicted corner and endpoint, then step through it. The first mismatch is a better place to investigate than the final miss alone.

## On the robot

Tape marks at the corners can make the first error visible on the mat. Measure that error before adjusting the next segment.

## Check and adjust

A route log should identify which segment first leaves the planned path. Retesting that segment on its own may show whether distance, turn angle, or launch pose caused the difference.
