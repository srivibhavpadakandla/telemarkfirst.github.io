# Lesson F8.3: Arcs and Speed Settings

An arc can round a corner that would otherwise need a straight segment and an in-place turn. `DriveBase.arc(radius, angle=...)` takes a radius in millimeters and an angle in degrees; a positive radius makes a right arc. We will compare two short arcs with a segmented route and watch the whole robot footprint, including its attachment.

## Worked example

The two arcs have equal radii with opposite signs. `settings()` also uses separate units for straight and turning motion: mm/s and mm/s², then deg/s and deg/s².

~~~python
robot.settings(160, 320, 90, 180)  # mm/s, mm/s², deg/s, deg/s²
robot.arc(100, angle=30)
robot.arc(-100, angle=30)
~~~

## Simulator practice

Try the worked example's two arcs in the editor, then compare them with a route made from straight segments and in-place turns. The comparison can show whether the curve gives enough clearance on this field.

## On the robot

A taped arc and the actual attachment can help reveal a clearance problem between the start and end points.

## Check and adjust

Record the radius and speed settings with their units. If the robot touches an obstacle during the arc, a wider radius or another route may leave more room.
